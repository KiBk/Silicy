## Ride notes

These are Kirill's personal recollections of this ride, not current route or service guarantees.

### 0 · Early October, at our own pace

The weather was mostly great; early October felt like a good time to ride. The proposed road route had far too much gravel for my road wheels—I had four punctures.

Most daily distances were just right. They looked short on paper, but rough roads, slow descents and plenty of climbing filled the days. We could wake up relaxed, stop to admire nature or solve problems, and still avoid rushing.

### 1 · A hectic start, a lovely finish

The short stretch from Palermo train station was chaotic: lots of people and cars, and a little scary for me. The pass pickup shop was closed, so we couldn't collect our passes—plan around its opening hours.

Gibellina was great: a nice hotel and plenty of dinner and supermarket options.

### 2 · Ruins all to ourselves

One of the highlights of the whole trip was wandering into the ruins. It was wonderful to walk around with nobody else there.

### 3 · Bring lights for the tunnel

The tunnel felt like a real adventure. Not knowing its length made it scary, although it turned out not to be very long. Bring lights.

This was also our first descent that felt like an abandoned, overgrown trail, leading toward the lake. The view afterwards was great.

### 4 · From green to gold

On the way to Montedoro, the surroundings changed noticeably from green vegetation to yellow sand. The climbs were quite hot, but nothing extreme.

### 5 · Thorns, then the climb to Enna

Another descent felt like an abandoned trail, with lots of thorn bushes—not ideal for tyres or exposed skin. The climb to Enna was tough but manageable.

### 6 · Finding our way around the gravel

The road to Regalbuto felt quite deserted. This was where we first started changing the route to avoid gravel.

We restocked inner tubes in Leonforte—the last bike shop we found on our way to Catania.

### 7 · A day that was too long

The ride to Lentini was too long for the relaxed pace we wanted. We felt rushed and stopped much less.

Catania was chaotic, and the roads afterwards felt boring and straight. This was the day I would make shorter.

### 8 · The finish I hoped for

The road to Modica was great: all asphalt, excellent surfaces, few cars and green, lovely scenery—a complete contrast to Catania.

I was very happy to finish in Modica, with its quieter pace and beautiful appearance.
