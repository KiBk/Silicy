01

Buccheri — Sicily's old ice trade

The story: High in the Hyblean Mountains, Buccheri once stored snow in neviere for the ice trade. Refrigeration made that mountain economy obsolete.

Notice: In town, Sant'Antonio's dramatic staircase. The surviving snow stores are scattered in the uplands, not all beside the cycling route.

Ride note: Town-area stop. Do not add a hunt for remote snow stores to an already long ride.

Maps · Chiesa di Sant'Antonio Abate, Buccheri

02

Giarratana — the quieter Hyblean village

The story: The old quarter, called Cuozzu, preserves a compact village setting associated with a route of rural-life house museums. It offers a small-scale contrast to the cities at either end of the day.

Notice: The old streets and modest domestic buildings, rather than searching for one huge monument.

Ride note: The course passes through Giarratana; exploring the Cuozzu quarter is optional. Check distance and energy before leaving the GPX.

Maps · Giarratana town centre, by the town hall

03

Modica — churches above the streets

The story: Modica's dramatic church stairways turn the hillside itself into architecture. San Giorgio and San Pietro are the big visual landmarks, rather than isolated monuments in flat squares.

Notice: San Giorgio from below. After a long ride, admiring the staircase is enough—you need not climb every step.

Ride note: Finish-city walk. The GPX ends in central Modica, not at Fausto's private address.

Maps · Duomo di San Giorgio, Modica

04

Modica chocolate — why it feels grainy

The story: Low-temperature processing leaves sugar crystals in Modica chocolate, giving it a deliberately grainy texture. That crunch is a feature, not a badly made smooth chocolate bar.

Notice: Compare a plain bar with cinnamon or vanilla versions if a shop offers a tasting.

Ride note: A relaxed finish-town reward. Shops, tastings and opening hours are not pre-booked.

Maps · Chocolate shops in central Modica
